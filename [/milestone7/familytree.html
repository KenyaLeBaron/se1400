What was the LeBaron Family Lineage like?

The LeBaron family tree is less a simple lineage and more a large, branching network of descendants tied to one patriarch, with divisions shaped by religion, migration, and internal conflict.

At its root is Alma Dayer LeBaron Sr., who moved his family from the United States to northern Mexico in the 1920s. He was part of a broader movement of Mormon settlers who left the U.S. to continue practicing plural marriage after it was rejected by the The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Because of polygamy, Alma Sr. had multiple wives and many children, which created a rapidly expanding family tree with numerous branches.

The most significant divisions in the family came through his sons. Among them, Joel LeBaron and Ervil LeBaron became central figures. Joel helped establish the Church of the Firstborn in the 1950s, positioning himself as a religious leader. Ervil later broke away, forming a rival faction and ordering violent attacks against perceived enemies, including members of his own family. These conflicts effectively split the family into separate religious and ideological lines, some of which became isolated or fragmented.

Other siblings and descendants did not follow these extremist paths. Over time, many LeBarons distanced themselves from fundamentalist groups altogether. As the generations expanded, the family spread across different communities in Mexico and the United States, creating a diverse extended network rather than a single unified group.

Today, the LeBaron family tree includes hundreds—if not thousands—of descendants, with varying beliefs and lifestyles. Some still live in Colonia LeBarón and nearby settlements, while others have moved elsewhere. The modern family includes farmers, business owners, activists, and professionals, many with dual U.S.-Mexican ties.

In short, the LeBaron family tree is defined by one common ancestor, but shaped by branching religious movements, internal divisions, and generations of growth that have turned it into a wide and complex extended family.
